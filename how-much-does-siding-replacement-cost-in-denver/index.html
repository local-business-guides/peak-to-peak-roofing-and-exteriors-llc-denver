What should siding replacement include?

A complete siding project starts with checking the existing exterior, then removing the siding included in the agreed scope. The contractor should inspect the exposed wall for damage, explain any needed repairs, and install the specified weather barrier, flashing, trim, and new siding details.

The quote should also explain how corners, windows, doors, utility penetrations, and transitions to the roof or foundation will be handled. Cleanup and disposal belong in the written scope too. Peak To Peak Roofing & Exteriors, LLC offers siding contractor services, along with roofing contractor and gutter service work, which can be useful when exterior details meet.

What does a local professional check before quoting?

A professional should look beyond the visible siding. They’ll check for loose or warped panels, failed caulking, damaged trim, signs of moisture, and how the siding meets windows, doors, roof edges, and gutters. The condition of the material underneath can’t always be confirmed until removal begins, so ask how concealed damage will be documented and priced.

The home’s size and shape matter, but so do the number of stories, access around the house, and the amount of cutting needed around openings and architectural details. A site visit also gives the contractor a chance to confirm which materials suit the home and to discuss any Denver neighborhood, historic district, or HOA requirements that may apply.

Which siding options should you compare?

Ask for options that fit your home, maintenance preferences, and budget rather than choosing by appearance alone. Each material has different installation requirements and upkeep, and the quote should identify the specific product and finish.

  • Vinyl siding is available in many styles and generally needs limited upkeep, but the product and installation quality still matter.
  • Fiber cement can offer a painted look and a solid feel, though it is heavier and needs correct handling and installation.
  • Engineered wood can provide a wood-like appearance. Ask about the product’s maintenance instructions and how exposed edges will be protected.
  • Natural wood offers a traditional look, but it typically needs more regular care to protect the finish and material.
  • Metal siding may suit some homes and design preferences. Confirm the finish, fastening details, and how the installer will handle dents or visible seams.

What drives the cost of siding replacement?

The main cost factors are the siding material, the amount of wall area, labor, removal and disposal, and the condition of the surface underneath. Complex shapes, multiple stories, tight access, and detailed trim or flashing work can add labor. If the crew finds rot or other damage after the siding comes off, repairs can change the final total.

Extras can include new trim, house wrap or other weather barrier, upgraded flashing, and changes around windows or doors. Ask whether each item is included, optional, or billed separately. A low quote may leave out work that another contractor has included, so compare the written scope line by line instead of comparing only the bottom line.

Denver homes vary in age, design, and exposure, so a price from another house is not a dependable estimate for yours. The contractor should explain the assumptions behind the quote and how any change in scope will be approved before additional work begins.

How can the project schedule change?

Timing depends on the project’s size and complexity, material availability, crew scheduling, weather, and whether repairs are needed after removal. The installation itself is only part of the schedule. Ordering the selected product and coordinating trim, flashing, or related exterior work can affect the start date.

Ask for an estimated start window, the expected work sequence, and how the company handles weather delays. If siding is removed in stages, ask how the exposed wall will be protected if conditions change. A contractor should explain what can affect the schedule rather than promise a fixed completion date before seeing the home.

What mistakes should you avoid?

  • Choosing a quote based only on its total without checking what work and materials it includes.
  • Assuming new siding will fix a moisture problem without finding and addressing its source.
  • Leaving disposal, trim, flashing, or cleanup details unclear in the contract.
  • Skipping a plan for unexpected damage, change orders, and approval of added work.
  • Ordering materials or starting work before checking applicable permits, HOA rules, or historic district requirements.

Questions to ask before hiring: Siding replacement checklist

  • What siding product, profile, and finish are included in the estimate?
  • Does the scope include removal, disposal, weather barrier, flashing, trim, and cleanup?
  • How will you inspect and document damage found after the existing siding is removed?
  • How are additional repairs priced, and will I approve them before work proceeds?
  • Who will supervise the project and be my main contact?
  • What is the expected start window, and what could delay the schedule?
  • Are permits or approvals needed for this home, and who will confirm that before work begins?

What is the next step?

Gather any prior inspection notes, photos of problem areas, and information about the siding you prefer, then arrange an on-site assessment. Request a written scope that identifies materials, removal, repairs, exclusions, payment terms, and how changes will be handled. Compare the details across quotes, and choose the provider whose plan you understand and feel comfortable approving.
