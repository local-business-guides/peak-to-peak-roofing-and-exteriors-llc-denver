What should a roofing job include?

A roofing contractor should inspect the roof, explain the recommended work, and give you a written estimate that describes the job. That estimate should make clear whether the work is a repair or replacement, which materials and areas are included, and what the crew will do with old roofing and debris.

Ask how the contractor will protect nearby landscaping and property, and how they will handle any damaged decking found after the old roof is removed. The final agreement should also explain payment terms, expected timing, warranty coverage, and who to contact if a concern comes up.

What will a local professional check?

A roofer should look beyond the spot where a leak appears. Water can travel under roofing before showing indoors, so the inspection should consider the roof surface and the details where different materials meet.

  • Shingles or other roof covering for missing, loose, cracked, or worn areas.
  • Flashing around chimneys, vents, skylights, and roof edges.
  • Valleys and drainage paths where water can collect or move quickly.
  • Gutters and downspouts for visible damage or blockages that affect drainage.
  • Signs of moisture inside, if accessible, and any roof decking concerns that can be confirmed safely.

Ask the roofer to show you what they found and explain what needs attention now versus what can be monitored. Photos can help you understand conditions you cannot see from the ground, but ask what each image shows rather than treating a photo alone as a diagnosis.

What changes the cost of roof work?

The estimate depends on the roof itself and the exact scope, not just the size of the home. Roof area, slope, layout, access, roofing material, the number of layers to remove, and the condition of the decking can all affect labor and materials.

Details such as flashing, vents, skylights, edge metal, and disposal should be accounted for in the written scope. A repair estimate may also change if inspection reveals damage beyond the visible leak. Ask the roofer to explain which items are included and how additional work will be approved before it begins.

When comparing estimates, check that each one covers the same areas and materials. If one is much lower, ask whether it excludes tear-off, replacement of damaged decking, cleanup, or other work included in the competing proposal. Don't rely on a verbal total when the written details are unclear.

How can Denver rules and weather affect the work?

Permit requirements can depend on the project and the local authority responsible for the property. Ask the roofer whether a permit applies, who will confirm the requirements, and who will arrange any required inspection. If your home is in a community with an HOA, check whether approval or material selection rules apply as well.

Weather can affect when crews can safely remove and install roofing, and a contractor may need to shift dates when conditions are unsuitable. Ask how the company will protect an exposed roof if work pauses, how it will let you know about schedule changes, and whether materials need to be selected or ordered in advance.

How do you compare timing and communication?

Ask for an expected start window and an estimate of how long the work may take, while understanding that weather, material availability, and hidden damage can change the schedule. A reliable plan explains who will update you and what happens if the crew finds a problem after work starts.

Before signing, agree on how extra work will be documented and approved. Find out whether you should be home, how the crew will access the property, and when the contractor expects to finish cleanup. Clear answers now make it easier to handle changes without confusion.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • What did you find during the inspection, and can you show me photos or explain the evidence?
  • Is repair a reasonable option, or are you recommending replacement? Why?
  • What materials and specific work are included in this written estimate?
  • Are tear-off, disposal, flashing, cleanup, and any likely decking work included or handled separately?
  • Are you insured for this work, and can you provide current documentation?
  • Will a permit or other approval be needed, and who will confirm and arrange it?
  • Who will supervise the job, and who should I contact with a concern?
  • How do you document and get approval for added work if hidden damage is found?

Which hiring mistakes should you avoid?

  • Choosing on price alone without checking that estimates cover the same work and materials.
  • Accepting a vague scope that does not say what will be removed, installed, or cleaned up.
  • Paying before you understand the payment schedule and how changes will be approved.
  • Skipping questions about insurance, permits, supervision, or written warranty terms.
  • Treating a suggested replacement as the only option without asking what a repair would address and what risks would remain.

Be cautious if a contractor pressures you to sign immediately, will not put the scope in writing, or cannot explain why the recommended work fits the roof's condition. Take time to compare proposals and confirm that the company has answered your questions plainly.
