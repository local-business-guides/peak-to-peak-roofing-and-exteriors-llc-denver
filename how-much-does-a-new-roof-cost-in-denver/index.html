What should a roof replacement estimate include?

A useful estimate starts with an inspection, not a guess based only on the home's square footage. The contractor should look at the roof's slope, layout, existing materials, flashing, ventilation, drainage, and any signs of damage below the surface.

The written proposal should spell out the work and materials so you can compare it with another bid. Look for details such as removal and disposal of the old roof, underlayment, flashing, cleanup, and how hidden damage will be handled if it appears after tear-off.

What does a new roof service involve?

A full replacement generally means removing the existing roofing material, checking the roof deck, repairing areas that need attention, and installing the new roofing system. The job may also involve replacing or updating flashing, underlayment, ventilation components, and edge details, depending on what the inspection finds.

Material choices can include asphalt shingles, metal roofing, or other systems suitable for the home. Each has different appearance, maintenance needs, installation requirements, and expected service life. Ask the contractor to explain which options suit your roof and to list the product specifications in the proposal.

Roofing options compared by type

  • Asphalt shingles: A common option for residential roofs, with a range of styles and product specifications. Compare the shingle product, underlayment, ventilation, and installation details in the estimate.
  • Metal roofing: Can have a different appearance and installation approach than shingles. Ask about the specific metal system, its details at roof transitions, and what maintenance to expect.
  • Other roofing systems: Some homes may suit other materials depending on roof design, local requirements, and the homeowner's priorities. Ask the contractor to explain the system's suitability, installation needs, and product information in writing.

The right choice depends on the home's roof, your budget, appearance preferences, and the product details in each proposal. Compare options by looking at the complete installed system, not just the surface material.

What drives the cost of a new roof in Denver?

The roof's area matters, but the home's living space alone doesn't tell you how much roofing is needed. A steep or complex roof can take more labor and material than a simple roof of similar area. Valleys, dormers, skylights, chimneys, and other transitions add details that need careful installation.

  • Roof size, pitch, shape, and ease of access.
  • Material type, product specifications, and the installation system it requires.
  • How many existing layers must be removed and disposed of.
  • The condition of the roof deck, flashing, ventilation, and drainage.
  • The amount of repair work or replacement of related components included.
  • Permits, disposal, site protection, and other items included in the contractor's scope.

Denver-area weather exposure can also affect the inspection and the materials a contractor recommends. If the estimate includes an allowance for concealed damage, ask what that allowance covers and how any additional work will be approved and priced before it proceeds.

What can affect the project schedule?

Timing depends on the roof's size and complexity, the crew's schedule, material availability, inspection or permit requirements, and weather. Rain, snow, high winds, or unsafe roof conditions can delay work. A contractor should explain how the crew will protect the home if the job has to pause.

Before signing, ask when work is expected to start, how long the crew expects to be on site, and how you'll be told about a delay. Don't treat a verbal target date as a guarantee unless the contract says what happens if the schedule changes.

What Denver rules and local details should you check?

Ask whether your roof replacement needs a permit and who will apply for it. Requirements can depend on the home's location and the work being done, so confirm the current rules with the relevant building department rather than relying on a general answer.

Also ask whether the proposal accounts for required inspections and any applicable product or installation requirements. If your home is in an association or has special property restrictions, check those rules before choosing materials or setting a start date.

What mistakes should you avoid before hiring?

  • Choosing a bid by its bottom line without checking whether the scope and materials match.
  • Assuming that damage beneath the shingles is included when the proposal does not say so.
  • Signing before you understand deposit terms, payment milestones, and how extra work is approved.
  • Relying on a spoken promise about cleanup, ventilation, flashing, or warranty coverage.
  • Treating a storm or insurance discussion as a reason to skip your own review of the contract.

Get important commitments in writing and keep a copy of the estimate, contract, product details, and any change orders. A clear paper trail helps both you and the contractor stay on the same page if the roof reveals problems during removal.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • What did you find during the inspection, and can you show me the areas that need work?
  • Which materials and installation details are included in this proposal?
  • Does the price include tear-off, disposal, flashing, ventilation work, and cleanup?
  • How will you document and get approval for repairs if hidden damage is found?
  • Who will supervise the job, and who should I contact with questions?
  • Do I need a permit, and who is responsible for checking and obtaining it?
  • What payment schedule, warranty information, and written change-order process apply?
  • How will weather or a material delay affect the schedule and protection of my home?
