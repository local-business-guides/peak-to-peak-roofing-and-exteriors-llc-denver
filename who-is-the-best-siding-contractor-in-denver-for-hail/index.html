What should a hail siding inspection cover?

A useful inspection looks beyond the most visible dents. The contractor should check each side of the home for cracks, holes, loose or split panels, damaged trim, and signs that water may have reached the layer beneath the siding.

Hail can hit the roof, gutters, downspouts, window and door trim, and siding in the same storm. Ask the contractor to note the locations and types of damage, take clear photos, and explain which findings are functional damage and which are cosmetic.

How can I tell if the siding needs repair or replacement?

The right scope depends on the siding material, how widespread the damage is, and whether the panels still protect the home. A few isolated damaged pieces may be repairable, while damage across an elevation or around vulnerable details may call for broader work.

Matching matters, too. Older siding colors and profiles can be hard to source, and a replacement section may look different from the existing wall. A contractor should explain the match before work starts and identify any trim, flashing, or underlayment that may need attention.

What affects the cost of hail-damaged siding work?

There is no reliable price to quote without seeing the home. Cost can change with the siding material and profile, the amount and location of damage, how many elevations need work, and whether trim, flashing, house wrap, or other concealed components are affected.

Material availability and color matching can also affect the recommended scope. Ask whether the estimate covers removal and disposal, replacement materials, labor, related trim work, cleanup, and any necessary protection for nearby surfaces. Compare written scopes line by line rather than comparing totals alone.

How do timing and weather affect the project?

Timing depends on the contractor's schedule, the size of the repair, material availability, and whether the inspection finds hidden damage. After a major hailstorm, many homeowners may be seeking inspections at once, so scheduling can take longer than it does during a quieter period.

Ask when the inspection can happen, how soon materials can be confirmed, and what could change the start date. Siding work also depends on suitable weather and safe working conditions, so get a realistic schedule rather than a promise that ignores possible delays.

What should I ask before I hire?

  • What hail damage did you find, and can you show me where it is?
  • Is the proposed work a repair, replacement of one section, or replacement of more siding? Why?
  • How will you check for damage behind the siding and around trim or flashing?
  • Can you provide a written scope listing materials, labor, cleanup, and any exclusions?
  • Can the new siding be matched to the existing material, color, and profile?
  • Who will be my project contact, and who will do the installation?
  • What could change the schedule or the final scope once work begins?
  • What warranty applies to the materials and installation, and where are its limits explained?

A clear answer should connect the observed damage to the work being proposed. If the estimate is vague, ask for more detail before signing or paying a deposit.

What mistakes should I avoid after a hailstorm?

  • Choosing a contractor only because they can start immediately or have the lowest total.
  • Agreeing to a broad replacement scope without photos and an explanation of the damage.
  • Assuming a siding repair estimate includes gutters, roof components, trim, or concealed layers.
  • Signing a contract before reading its scope, payment terms, cancellation terms, and warranty details.
  • Treating a contractor's opinion as a guarantee of what an insurance policy will cover.

Keep copies of inspection photos, estimates, and messages. If you plan to contact your insurer, ask the insurer about your policy and claim process directly; a contractor's inspection and an insurer's coverage decision are not the same thing.

When should I call a siding professional?

Call for an inspection if you see cracked or punctured panels, loose siding, damaged corners or trim, or signs of water getting inside. It is also sensible to have the exterior checked when a hailstorm has visibly damaged nearby roofs, gutters, or other exposed surfaces, even if the siding damage is not obvious from the ground.

If water is entering the home, tell the contractor when you call and ask what immediate steps are appropriate. Avoid climbing onto a roof or removing siding to investigate; a professional can check the exterior safely and explain what needs attention.
